========================================================================
  UPLOAD GUIDE  -  getting the best quality on YouTube & Instagram
========================================================================

THE SHORT VERSION
  YouTube and Instagram RE-COMPRESS every video you upload - there's no way
  to stop that, and it happens to everyone. The creators whose videos look
  amazing aren't avoiding it; they hand the platform a file that survives it.
  This guide + the new  Run-ExportForUpload.bat  button do exactly that.

  Biggest wins, in order:
    1. Instagram: turn ON "Upload at highest quality" (it's OFF by default!)
    2. Film + upload in the HIGHEST resolution you can (4K if your phone does it)
    3. Upload the clean master file (output\upload\), not a re-shared copy
    4. Upload on strong Wi-Fi, and don't touch the app until it finishes
    5. Post vertical 9:16 for Shorts/Reels (don't let them letterbox you)


------------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 0  -  MAKE THE MASTER FILE  (this tool)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  After you've cleaned / trimmed / captioned, run:
        Run-ExportForUpload.bat
  It writes upload-ready files to  output\upload\ , encoded to exactly what
  YouTube and Instagram recommend:
     - H.264 High profile, yuv420p, fast-start (their preferred format)
     - AAC audio, 48 kHz, 384 kbps
     - CRF 18 master quality (clean data for their encoder to work from)
     - your original resolution + aspect kept (no cropping)

  Upload the file from  output\upload\  - NOT a copy that's been AirDropped,
  WhatsApp'd, or re-saved (those get compressed before you even upload).


------------------------------------------------------------------------
  INSTAGRAM REELS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1) TURN ON HIGH-QUALITY UPLOADS  (do this once - it's the #1 fix)
       Profile -> the (three-line) menu -> Settings and privacy
         -> Account (or "Media quality" / "Data usage")
         -> turn ON  "Upload at highest quality"
     It is OFF by default, which is why Reels look soft for many people.

  2) UPLOAD CONDITIONS
     - Use strong Wi-Fi (not cellular / weak hotel Wi-Fi). Instagram
       compresses harder when the upload is slow or unstable.
     - Don't lock the phone or switch apps mid-upload. Keep Instagram open
       until the Reel has fully finished processing.
     - A Reel can look soft for the first ~30 min after posting - Instagram
       finishes the high-quality version in the background. Check again later
       before deciding it's bad.

  3) FILE (already handled if you use output\upload)
     - 1080 x 1920, 9:16, 30fps, H.264 + AAC.
     - Instagram's sweet spot is a MODERATE bitrate (~8-12 Mbps at 1080).
       A crazy-high bitrate can actually trigger HARDER recompression. If you
       ever want to be safe, make an Instagram-tuned file:
           .\Export-ForUpload.ps1 -MaxBitrateM 12


------------------------------------------------------------------------
  YOUTUBE (Shorts and regular)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1) UPLOAD IN HIGH RESOLUTION - this is the big one
     YouTube gives higher-resolution uploads its BETTER codec (VP9/AV1),
     while plain 1080p often gets basic H.264. So:
       - If your phone films in 4K, KEEP it 4K all the way through and upload
         4K. This is the single biggest quality lever for Shorts. (Set your
         phone camera to 4K; this tool preserves it.)
       - Shorts still DISPLAY at 1080, but a 4K-sourced Short looks noticeably
         sharper and cleaner (less blockiness in dark/motion) after YouTube's
         re-encode.

     Only filmed in 1080p? You can UPSCALE the master to 4K to nudge YouTube
     toward the better codec (widely used trick; costs upload time, quality
     gain varies):
           .\Export-ForUpload.ps1 -FourK
     Real 4K footage always beats an upscale - so prefer filming in 4K.

  2) FILE (already handled if you use output\upload)
     - H.264 High profile, MP4, fast-start, AAC 48 kHz 384 kbps.
     - YouTube's own recommended delivery bitrates: 1080p30 = 8 Mbps,
       1080p60 = 12 Mbps, 4K30 = 35-45 Mbps. Our CRF-18 master sits above
       these on purpose, so the re-encode has headroom.

  3) UPLOAD OVER WI-FI and let it finish processing. Right after upload
     YouTube may only have the low-res version ready for a few minutes - give
     it time before judging quality, and watch it back at full resolution
     (set the player gear to the highest number).


------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FILMING TIPS (they matter more than any software step)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Set your phone camera to 4K (or the highest it offers). More real detail
    captured = more that survives compression. This is the biggest lever.
  - Film VERTICAL for Shorts/Reels (turn the phone upright) so the video
    fills the 9:16 screen. A landscape clip posted to Reels gets shrunk into
    a small strip or cropped - which looks like "bad quality" but is really
    the wrong shape.
  - Good, even light = less noise. Compression turns noise/grain into mush,
    so bright, clean footage always compresses better.
  - Keep the lens clean and hold steady (or use a small tripod). Motion blur
    and shake give the encoder more to throw away.
